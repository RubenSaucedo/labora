# Summary altitude and placement

Every example here is synthetic. Never copy a technology, metric, scope or outcome from this file into a resume.

## The mission

The Summary gives the reader the high-level interpretation of the resume. It establishes identity, level, relevant scope and differentiated value. Experience and Projects prove that proposition; Technical Skills makes exact technologies retrievable.

A Summary can be accurate, concise and still spend the most-read sentence on implementation detail. The defect is placement, not truth.

## Six editing rules

1. Do not use the Summary as a compressed Skills section.
2. Before keeping a technology or mechanism, ask whether it changes the person's professional identity or is merely evidence for it.
3. When a term creates technical ambiguity, first test whether it belongs in the Summary at all. Do not default to stacking qualifiers.
4. Prefer confirmed system scope, lifecycle ownership, production responsibility, organisational leverage, or independently inspectable work.
5. Keep exact mechanism, modality and protocol names in Experience bullets unless one is central to the target role and genuinely differentiating.
6. Check the headline, Technical Skills and body before repeating a keyword.

## Raise ambiguous mechanisms

Synthetic:

> Built a response-synthesis layer.

The instinct is to narrow it:

> Built a text-based response-synthesis layer.

That is more precise and still at the wrong altitude. The repair is to raise:

> Led agent work from architecture through production.

The exact modality and mechanism then live in an Experience bullet, where they are the point.

## The coverage trap

Synthetic posting terms: adapter contracts, protocol integrations, concurrency, graceful degradation, latency and observability. A Summary that tries to carry them all reads assembled from retrieval terms:

> Software engineer building registry-backed adapter contracts, protocol integrations, request-scoped concurrency, graceful degradation, measured latency improvements, and observability.

Even if every noun is true, the sentence has replaced career scope with mechanisms. Put retrieval terms where they serve the reader.

## What the Summary must not borrow

Seniority, scale, adoption and timing words read as ordinary resume register, so they are easy to copy from a posting: `real-time`, `at scale`, `production-scale`, `widely adopted`, `Senior`. Each is an assertion. Ask whether the person confirms that exact scope before using it.

## Do not generalise away the specialisation

Raising altitude is not a licence to become vague. If a technology is the target specialisation, naming it in the Summary may be the correct altitude because it is the professional identity, not merely a mechanism.
